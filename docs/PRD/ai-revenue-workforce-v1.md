# AI Revenue Workforce V1

**Status:** Product-definition draft
**Product:** Event Suite
**Territories:** South Africa, United Kingdom, United States
**Primary motion:** Direct sales
**Decision this PRD supports:** How Event Suite reliably creates and converts demand, and what work the AI workforce may safely own. Cross-market performance is a secondary allocation signal.

## 1. Product outcome

The AI Revenue Engine creates a supervised revenue workforce for Event Suite. It converts public market signals, first-party demand and commercial evidence into prioritised work, safe customer communication, qualified opportunities and measurable learning.

The V1 outcome is not “maximum leads” or “maximum emails sent.” It is repeatable Event Suite revenue: qualified conversations that become opportunities, conversions and retained revenue at acceptable cost and quality.

The workforce operates to sell. Its market comparison is a by-product of instrumented sales activity, used to improve allocation, proposition and execution; it must never prevent the pursuit or expansion of a credible revenue opportunity.

Commercial progress is a sequence of evidence-backed outcomes: qualified account, legitimate buyer route, useful interaction, qualified conversation, opportunity, conversion and retained revenue signal. A high volume of weak accounts, unapproved messages or unqualified meetings is not success.

V1 records a strategic inclusion hypothesis: Event Suite may find early traction with community-led, grassroots, regional, independent and otherwise underserved event operators that are often missed by enterprise-oriented event technology sales. It does not test that hypothesis in the first cross-market cohort, because varying both territory and vertical would make the early result ambiguous. These attributes are retained as subgroup tags for later evidence-backed vertical tests.

A community-led event is not a size class. It may be local or national, small or large, volunteer-led or professionally staffed. The workforce records vertical/operating model, event or organisation scale, geography and commercial evidence as separate dimensions; none may be inferred from another.

## 2. Scope

### In scope

- Event Suite Direct in South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States.
- Comparable market research, target discovery, account identity, commercial research and contact research.
- Marketing planning and content recommendations connected to specific audience, problem and commercial programme.
- Supervised outbound prospecting, inbound lead triage, response classification and opportunity coordination.
- Shared commercial memory: accounts, contacts, evidence, interactions, decisions, opportunities, outcomes and commercial-performance metrics.
- Human approvals, provenance, suppression and auditable agent handoffs.
- Cross-market learning that improves prioritisation, messaging, channel mix and future investment without becoming a quota or a brake on sales.

### Out of scope for V1

- Fully autonomous contract negotiation, pricing exceptions or material customer commitments.
- Bulk or high-volume autonomous outreach.
- Scraping protected systems, private data collection, inferred personal data or guessed contact details.
- Local Network Operator recruitment as part of the three-market commercial programme.
- Allxs and Prestige ID commercial execution.
- Treating the engine as a replacement for a full customer-support platform or a general-purpose CRM.

## 3. Core product principles

1. **Revenue outcomes over activity.** Optimise for qualified opportunities and learning, not records, messages or superficial engagement.
2. **Evidence before persuasion.** Facts, inferences and unknowns remain separate. Agents never invent pain, relationships, contacts, product capability or commercial claims.
3. **One shared commercial memory.** Accounts, people, evidence, opportunities, activities and outcomes are reusable across Event Suite products and future products.
4. **Measurement serves selling.** Territory, ICP, wedge, channel and cost are recorded so performance can improve. Comparison must not override credible revenue opportunity, safety or local commercial reality.
5. **Autonomy is earned.** External actions expand only after quality, safety and commercial-value evidence is demonstrated.
6. **Humans own commitments.** AI may prepare, prioritise, coordinate and recommend. Humans own approval of consequential communication, commercial terms, negotiations and exceptions.
7. **A safe unresolved outcome is valuable.** The system should prefer `UNKNOWN`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED` or `BLOCKED` to a confident but unsupported action.
8. **Inclusion is testable, not assumed.** Community, grassroots, regional and underserved attributes are retained for analysis, while priority depends on the active ICP, evidence, receptivity and commercial progress rather than event size, status or a demographic proxy.
9. **Provider coverage is not market receptivity.** A missing Apollo result may reflect the provider's coverage of a territory, vertical or organisation type. It does not mean there is no buyer, no contact route, no Event Suite opportunity or no value in the market.

## 4. Commercial programme and performance measurement

### 4.1 V1 programme matrix

| Dimension | V1 rule |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Event Suite |
| Sales motion | Direct |
| Territories | ZA, UK, US |
| Customer cohort | Independent organisers and promoters of recurring, ticketed events, with a clear commercial owner, active public event evidence, and an observable growth or operational problem; community-led and other event attributes are recorded as subgroups, not initial cohort quotas |
| Primary conversion | Owner-approved self-service or qualified live-demo route; route must be recorded per account |
| Exclusions | Event Suite first party, competitors, blocked/suppressed accounts, unclear identity, unsupported territory or prohibited source |
| Unit of learning | A resolved, evidence-backed account progressing through a recorded commercial stage |

The initial direct-sales programme excludes Local Network Operator recruitment, schools-specific pricing and other special programmes from its standard performance view unless they are deliberately operated as their own commercial programme.

### 4.1a Discovery tags and later vertical tests

The workforce must explore a broad event economy and retain the attributes needed to examine underserved event operators without allowing an analysis category to override sales evidence. Discovery tags include:

- community-led, grassroots and neighbourhood events;
- regional and smaller-town festivals, fairs, cultural events and recurring local programmes;
- independent organisers and promoters;
- associations, charities and institutions with genuine recurring event operations;
- independent venues operating meaningful owned programmes;
- established commercial, conference, exhibition, sport and entertainment operators.

Vertical/operating model and size are independent fields. For example, a large community festival remains community-led; a small commercial conference remains commercial. The engine must not translate a community label into `PHASE_ONE_SME`, or use event scale as a proxy for community relevance.

`Underserved` is a commercial and product-access hypothesis, not a claim about a person or community. It must be supported by observable event-operating evidence such as a fragmented owned presence, manual coordination burden, limited fit from existing tools, a small team carrying disproportionate work, or an unmet discoverability/operations need. The system must not infer it from race, ethnicity, income, geography, event size or other sensitive or demographic proxies.

These are descriptive tags, not protected quotas or automatic inclusion rules. They are reported independently. A dedicated vertical analysis may begin when there is enough volume to make it useful, but it must not delay pursuit of an eligible account. No vertical is rejected because it is small, local or hard to discover.

### 4.2 Controlled comparison rules

The three markets must be measured well enough to improve allocation. The workforce must:

- use the same product family and core value hypotheses unless a documented territory adaptation is needed;
- set a commercial budget and capacity limit for each territory; any matched comparison sample is analytical only;
- use the same qualification taxonomy, evidence threshold, activity statuses and conversion definitions;
- preserve territory-specific pricing, language, business practice, consent and channel restrictions rather than pretending the markets are identical;
- record every local adaptation, including message, offer, route, price presentation, channel or approval difference;
- compare like with like when making a market claim: same ICP, source class, channel, sequence stage and elapsed time;
- retain a comparable measurement subset where useful, but never cap, delay or exclude an eligible revenue opportunity to preserve it;
- report subgroup outcomes and never declare a market winner from raw lead volume alone.

### 4.3 Revenue operating periods and decision cadence

A revenue operating period is an active commercial programme with an approved budget, policy and measurement plan. Before activation, the commercial owner sets:

- eligible-account capacity and commercial budget per territory;
- maximum human-reviewed messages and provider spend per territory;
- active ICP, exclusions and any comparable measurement subset;
- permitted channels and message variants;
- primary conversion event;
- observation window;
- safety and quality thresholds.

The initial supervised period should be small enough to review every account and message manually. A territory may receive more scale because it produces qualified opportunities or revenue evidence; it must not receive more scale merely because it produces more easy-to-find public signals.

At the end of each operating period, the Revenue Director produces one of four commercial allocation decisions for every territory:

- **Advance:** continue or increase the programme where qualified opportunity and revenue evidence support it.
- **Adapt:** retain the market but change one explicit variable, such as ICP, message, offer or route.
- **Hold:** insufficient evidence; do not add spend until the missing evidence is available.
- **Deprioritise:** weaker early signal at comparable effort and quality; retain the evidence but stop allocating the next expansion cohort.

## 5. Revenue lifecycle

```text
MARKET / ICP HYPOTHESIS
  -> DISCOVER AND RESOLVE ACCOUNT
  -> BUILD COMMERCIAL EVIDENCE
  -> QUALIFY AND PRIORITISE
  -> SELECT MARKETING OR SALES ACTION
  -> HUMAN-APPROVED EXTERNAL COMMUNICATION
  -> CLASSIFY RESPONSE OR INBOUND INTENT
  -> PROGRESS QUALIFIED OPPORTUNITY
  -> RECORD OUTCOME, COST AND LEARNING
  -> UPDATE NEXT MARKET ROUND
```

Every transition must preserve the originating territory, commercial programme, evidence basis, owner, timestamps and reason for the action or non-action.

## 6. Workforce roles

The workforce is a set of bounded roles sharing one commercial memory. It is not a set of independent chat personas. Individual human approval is a temporary testing control; the intended operating state is autonomous execution inside an earned policy boundary, with humans resolving exceptions and changing the policy.

| Role | Primary job | May create | Must not decide alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue Director | Set commercial priorities, allocation and performance reporting | commercial programme plan, allocation recommendation, performance decision | territory activation, budget expansion, pricing or strategy changes |
| Market Intelligence | Map active market signals, sectors, ICP evidence and competitors | market/segment evidence | a target is sales-ready or a competitor claim is approved |
| Discovery & Identity Team | Find credible signals and resolve the commercial organisation | candidate, identity graph, validated account | qualification, contactability or outreach |
| Commercial Researcher | Diagnose evidence-backed Event Suite opportunities and counter-evidence | commercial brief, opportunity hypotheses, unknowns | unsupported pain, product promise or sales approval |
| Marketing Planner | Turn proven audience problems into campaign, content and resource recommendations | campaign brief, content brief, nurture recommendation | publish externally or make unapproved product/competitive claims |
| Buyer & Contact Researcher | Find a legitimate public route to the likely problem owner | contact route with provenance | guessed contacts, enrichment outside policy or outreach |
| Sales Engagement | Prepare and, once promoted, execute a narrow evidence-grounded sequence | draft/approved sequence, reply classification, next-action recommendation | send outside the approved audience, channel, message, rate or stop policy |
| Inbound & Response Agent | Triage inbound intent, execute approved responses and route exceptions | classification, approved response, owner queue, next-action recommendation | promise pricing, custom scope, demos or commercial terms outside policy |
| Opportunity Manager | Keep qualified conversations moving with clear next actions | opportunity brief, meeting preparation, follow-up task, risk flag | negotiation, contract changes or exceptions outside approved commercial policy |
| RevOps & Learning | Maintain data quality, measurement, attribution and performance analysis | dashboard, market comparison, quality alert | alter source evidence, suppress a record without policy basis or silently reclassify outcomes |
| Human Commercial Owner | Approve commitments and resolve exceptions | approvals, commercial decisions, feedback | delegate prohibited decisions without changing policy |

### Current implementation boundary

The implemented foundation covers the Discovery & Identity Team, Commercial Researcher, Buyer & Contact Researcher, parts of Sales Engagement, operator review and inbound-lead triage. The Revenue Director, Marketing Planner, Response Agent, Opportunity Manager and RevOps & Learning roles are requirements for future delivery, not claims of completed automation.

## 7. Authority and approval model

| Level | Permitted action | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0 — Observe | Read, normalise, classify and recommend | evidence extraction, lead categorisation, research gaps |
| A1 — Draft | Create internal work for review | account brief, message draft, campaign brief, meeting prep |
| A2 — Internal execute | Update approved internal records with an audit trail | tasks, activity log, queue priority, approved status transition |
| A3a — Supervised external execute | Take one bounded external action after record-level approval | send one approved message to one verified route |
| A3b — Monitored autonomous external execute | Execute a policy-approved external action without record-level approval | send an approved sequence to an eligible verified route; issue a pre-approved inbound acknowledgement; schedule an approved follow-up |
| A4 — Exception and commitment | Handle actions outside approved policy | price exception, contract, negotiation, strategic partnership, major relationship |

V1 uses A0–A2 and A3a to create a labelled quality baseline. A workflow may progress to A3b only after it passes the Market Test Operating Protocol: stable policy compliance, no unresolved safety defects, reliable evidence and contact gates, acceptable commercial quality, auditable delivery/reply outcomes, rate limits, reversible stop controls and a named owner for the policy. A4 remains exception-only; it is not the default operating path for ordinary qualified sales work.

## 8. Marketing and sales workflows

### 8.1 Account-based outbound

1. Discovery finds a candidate in a declared territory cohort.
2. Identity and commercial gates establish whether the account belongs in the test.
3. The workforce selects one evidence-backed problem and one credible action.
4. Sales Engagement drafts a message or recommends a marketing resource.
5. During the proving phase, a human approves, revises, rejects or blocks the action. Once promoted, an eligible action executes autonomously under its policy version and audit rules.
6. A response is classified as no response, route-to-buyer, interest, objection, disqualification, opt-out, customer/partner/competitor or other. Approved low-risk response classes may receive an autonomous policy response; exceptions are routed to a human.
7. A qualified response becomes an owned opportunity with a next action and due date.
8. The outcome updates the commercial-performance record.

### 8.2 Inbound demand

1. An Event Suite form, resource request, trial, demo request or approved inbound source creates immutable source evidence.
2. The Inbound & Response Agent classifies intent, consent treatment, urgency and data gaps.
3. The system resolves shared account/contact context without unsafe merges.
4. A human owner receives a clear next action during testing. Once a treatment is promoted, the agent may perform its approved acknowledgement, routing or follow-up without individual approval and escalates only exceptions.
5. Outcome and source are retained for attribution and market learning.

### 8.3 Marketing loop

1. Market Intelligence and sales evidence identify recurring audience problems, questions and objections.
2. Marketing Planner proposes an audience, message, content/resource and distribution hypothesis.
3. A human approves external content, claims, campaign rules and spend during testing. Once a campaign pattern is promoted, the Marketing Planner may execute within the approved audience, assets, budget, channel and stop policy.
4. Marketing performance is connected to attributable inbound activity, qualified conversations and opportunities rather than impressions alone.
5. The Revenue Director compares the result with outbound performance and adjusts the next operating period.

## 9. Commercial memory and integrations

The Revenue Engine is the commercial-intelligence and workflow system of record. It shares narrowly scoped facts with other systems; it does not require unrestricted access to their databases.

V1 records at minimum:

- commercial programme, territory, ICP cohort, commercial operating period and comparison subset where applicable;
- account, organisation relationship, contact route and suppression state;
- source evidence, confidence, freshness, facts, inferences and unknowns;
- product opportunity, commercial hypothesis, counter-evidence and next action;
- messages, approvals, replies, inbound source, activities and opportunity stages;
- owner, timestamps, cost, provider use and quality flags;
- conversion outcome, revenue outcome where available and reason for loss/deprioritisation.

### 9.1 People-data coverage and contactability

Apollo is the primary bounded people-matching provider, not the measure of a market's contactability or commercial potential. The engine must record the reason that people matching did or did not produce a usable candidate, including at minimum: not eligible to query, not queried because of budget/policy, provider unavailable/error, zero provider match, candidate found but no verified route, and verified route found.

A zero or unavailable provider result keeps the account's commercial evidence intact and leaves the contact state unresolved. It must not downgrade the account, event, vertical, territory or product hypothesis. The system must not use a provider-coverage gap as a reason to invent a contact or to expand into unbounded personal-data collection.

Market reports must make the distinction visible between account quality, Apollo coverage, verified contactability and commercial progression. This allows the team to decide whether a weak result reflects poor demand, the wrong vertical, weak source coverage, an unsuitable outreach route or an incomplete commercial proposition.

Future integrations must have an explicit owner and contract:

| Integration | Purpose | V1 rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event Suite | inbound intent, trial/demo signals and eventual product outcomes | server-to-server, minimum necessary facts |
| Email provider | approved delivery, reply and engagement events | no activation until sender, consent, suppression, approval and stop controls are proven |
| Calendar/meeting system | scheduled conversation and outcome context | human-controlled connection; no autonomous bookings in V1 |
| CRM/contract system | opportunity and commercial outcome | shared identifiers and auditable updates; do not create a parallel uncontrolled pipeline |
| Research providers | bounded identity, company and contact evidence | source-specific authority, cost controls and provenance required |

## 10. Measurement and decision rules

### 10.1 Guardrail metrics

Every territory is measured for:

- identity-resolution precision and duplicate rate;
- evidence completeness and unsupported-claim rate;
- human approval, revision, rejection and block rate;
- contact-route validity;
- people-provider coverage, zero-match, unavailable and verified-route rates;
- opt-out, complaint and suppression incidents;
- cost per resolved account, qualified account and meaningful interaction;
- time from signal to owner action;
- provider and model spend per commercial outcome.

Any material safety failure pauses the affected action type in the affected territory. Raw message volume can never compensate for a safety or evidence-quality failure.

### 10.2 Commercial metrics

The engine compares each territory using consistent definitions:

- qualified-account rate from the active ICP and, where present, the comparable subset;
- legitimate-contact-route rate;
- approved-message-to-meaningful-reply rate;
- meaningful-reply-to-qualified-conversation rate;
- qualified-conversation-to-opportunity rate;
- opportunity-to-conversion rate;
- time to first qualified conversation;
- attributable pipeline and revenue, where the sales cycle permits;
- cost per qualified opportunity and cost per conversion;
- human review time per qualified opportunity.

Every measure must be available by `territory × commercial programme × ICP × wedge × channel × operating period`, with vertical, operating model, community status and event/organisation scale available as independent subgroup tags. The Revenue Director first uses the data to improve revenue allocation; a like-for-like comparison subset supports a market-receptivity claim when available. A dedicated vertical analysis may identify a receptive vertical once enough activity exists. A low provider-match rate is a data-coverage finding until commercial evidence proves otherwise.

Early operating periods may not contain enough closed revenue to identify a durable winner. In that case, the leading indicator is the highest-quality progression to a qualified conversation or opportunity at acceptable cost, not superficial engagement.

### 10.3 Attribution rules

- Every activity records territory, commercial programme, cohort, channel, message/content version and campaign where applicable.
- An opportunity records its first attributable signal and all material assisting activities.
- First-touch, qualified-conversation and opportunity-creation attribution remain separate; one metric must not overwrite another.
- A human may correct attribution with a reason; agents do not silently rewrite commercial history.

## 11. Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — Align the operating contract

Make the PRD, territory playbooks, product claims, activity taxonomy, data model and current implementation status agree. Create the US Direct playbook before external activation. Define the first sales focus, commercial budget, policy boundaries and performance cadence.

### Phase 1 — Supervised three-market revenue operations

Run active account research, qualification and approved outreach in ZA, UK and US to pursue eligible revenue opportunities. Capture community-led and other event attributes as subgroup tags, but do not make them automatic inclusion rules or quotas. Human review remains mandatory. The output is qualified pipeline, an evidence-quality report, commercial-performance baseline and exploratory subgroup insight.

### Phase 2 — Supervised demand and engagement pilot

Enable approved outreach drafts and inbound triage for one channel per territory. Capture replies and inbound outcomes. Individual approval creates the quality baseline; no autonomous sending, meeting booking, pricing or negotiation occurs in this phase.

### Phase 3 — Opportunity operating loop

Add response intelligence, owned opportunity stages, meeting preparation, follow-up coordination, CRM synchronisation and loss/win learning. Continue human control of external commitments.

### Phase 4 — Marketing and monitored autonomy

Add approved campaign/content workflows, attribution and revenue learning. Promote narrow A3b actions only after territory-specific quality, consent, suppression, audit and commercial thresholds are proven. Autonomous actions remain bounded by policy, rate, channel, audience, offer and stop rules; the system escalates exceptions rather than silently improvising.

## 12. V1 acceptance criteria

The V1 workforce design is ready to begin Phase 1 when:

- ZA, UK and US each have a declared Event Suite Direct commercial programme and territory playbook status;
- the active ICP, commercial budget, observation window and primary conversion definition are recorded before research begins, with a comparison subset only where useful;
- vertical/operating model, scale and people-provider outcome can be reported independently for every candidate;
- current discovery, identity, commercial-research, contact and outreach gates remain intact;
- every candidate, decision and activity can be assigned to territory, programme and commercial operating period;
- human reviewers can approve, reject, block or request revision with a recorded reason;
- no external message can be sent without the applicable activation, identity, eligibility, contact, suppression and approval gates;
- commercial-performance reports show quality, cost, progression, revenue and safety metrics by territory;
- the end-of-period allocation decision is Advance, Adapt, Hold or Deprioritise, with supporting evidence.

## 13. Explicit open decisions

These need owner approval before Phase 1 external activation; the PRD deliberately does not guess them:

1. The final inclusion/exclusion rules and commercial wedge for the shared ICP in ZA, UK and US, plus the subgroup-tag taxonomy for exploratory analysis.
2. The account, provider-cost and human-review budget per territory per operating period.
3. The primary conversion event for the first operating period: self-service activation, qualified live demo, or another route.
4. The approved US pricing, claim and routing playbook.
5. The permissible first external channel in each territory.
6. Territory-specific consent, privacy, sender-identity and suppression requirements.
7. The human commercial owner and service-level expectation for inbound/high-intent responses.
8. The minimum people-data coverage evidence and approved unresolved-contact policy before a territory can be judged commercially weak.
